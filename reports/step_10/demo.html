Are the gaps clear? Series bootstrap, 95% interval

Shaded intervals exclude zero. Negative favors the first-named run.

The conflict rule changes no outcome when everything is approved, so "Agent" and "Agent with conflict rule" match. It matters only when a person can decline, as in the decline run. Human-review items are high volume, so declining about 1% to 2% of cases raises unmet demand sharply.

LLM sample

100 replayed cases, 25 per category. The LLM reviewed saved states afterward and did not drive the replay, so the costs above do not reflect its choices.

Effect on decisions

Both changes moved monitor to human review: one read a 0.4% risk as 40%, and one never gave a valid answer.

Explanation audit

Narrow rules, so these counts are a lower bound. Cases can break more than one.

What this does not show

  • One simulator. Supplier delays, inventory and costs are assumptions, so this measures no real savings.
  • Expediting is assumed to halve the remaining wait. With a 25% cut instead, the agent's unmet demand nearly doubles on baseline.
  • A lost sale counts only its margin. No lost customers or penalties are included, which favors the cheaper runs.
  • The approving person is simulated. A real reviewer might decline weak cases, which was not tested beyond the sensitivity runs.
  • The agent sits on the reorder-point routine. It can only add to it. Combining it with forecast-driven ordering was not tested.
  • The LLM is untuned, since Step 9 was skipped, and critical recall stays low at 0.41.
  • Tests: 469 of 469 passed. All 21 runs were not repeated end to end. Full report: reports/step_10/summary.md.
